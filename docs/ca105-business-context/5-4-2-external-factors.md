# 5.4.2 

## External factors that trigger change in organisations

This section covers the external factors that drive organisational change, including political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental influences.

---

## Political

### Change in government  
### Conflict  
### Shift in government priorities  

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Alters regulatory direction and funding priorities  
- Can introduce instability or new operational requirements  

**Benefits**
- May create new opportunities through policy change  
- Can stimulate innovation or sector investment  

**Drawbacks**
- Uncertainty in planning  
- Rapid changes to compliance requirements  
- Disruption to supply chains or services  

---

## Economic

### Provision of new services  
### Recession  
### Inflation  
### Interest rates  
### Consumer trends  
### New competitors  
### Entering new markets  

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Influences spending power, investment, and demand  
- Shapes competitive pressure and growth strategy  

**Benefits**
- Expansion into new markets can increase revenue  
- Consumer trends can guide service development  

**Drawbacks**
- Reduced budgets during recession  
- Increased operating costs due to inflation  
- Pressure from competitors can reduce market share  

---

## Social

### Changes in demographics  
### Market/social trends  
### Adapting to remote working  
### Cultural expectations  

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Affects workforce structure and user expectations  
- Drives demand for flexible and accessible digital systems  

**Benefits**
- Supports wider reach and inclusivity  
- Enables new working practices  

**Drawbacks**
- Requires adaptation of systems and processes  
- Skills gaps may emerge  
- Increased complexity in user support  

---

## Technological

### Emergence of new technologies  
### Retirement of obsolete technologies  
### System failure  
### Zero-day vulnerabilities  

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Forces upgrades or replacement of systems  
- Introduces new risks and opportunities  

**Benefits**
- Improved performance and capability  
- Access to innovative tools and platforms  

**Drawbacks**
- High implementation costs  
- Increased security exposure  
- Dependence on specialist skills  

---

## Legal

### New legislation  
### Changes to legislation  

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Introduces mandatory compliance requirements  
- Impacts data handling, accessibility, and operational processes  

**Benefits**
- Improves standards and accountability  
- Protects users and organisations  

**Drawbacks**
- Increased administrative burden  
- Cost of compliance and system changes  

---

## Environmental

### Sustainability issues  
### Pandemics  
### Natural disasters  

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Affects physical operations and digital resilience  
- Drives remote working and sustainability initiatives  

**Benefits**
- Encourages environmentally responsible practices  
- Accelerates adoption of flexible digital systems  

**Drawbacks**
- Operational disruption  
- Unexpected costs  
- Increased demand for resilient infrastructure  

---

## Judgement

Suitability depends on:

i. stability of the external environment  
ii. organisational capacity to adapt to economic and social change  
iii. readiness to adopt or replace technologies  
iv. legal and regulatory compliance requirements  
v. resilience to environmental disruption  

External factors introduce uncertainty and compel organisations to modify systems, processes, and strategy. Digital software development decisions must balance opportunity against constraints such as cost, compliance, technical capability, and operational continuity.

---

!!! tip "Exam tip"
    Avoid listing external factors without explanation. Marks are awarded for linking each factor to organisational impact and suitability. Responses must connect political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental change to constraints and outcomes before making judgement.
