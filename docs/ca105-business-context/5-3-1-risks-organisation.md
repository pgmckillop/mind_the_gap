## Potential risks to organisations when using digital systems

This section covers the potential risks faced by organisations when adopting and operating digital systems, including security, privacy, compliance, audience exclusion, competitive pressures, and technical failure.

---

## Security breaches

### Compromised confidentiality  
### Loss of integrity  
### Reduced availability  

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Unauthorised access to systems or data  
- Modification or corruption of information  
- Disruption to services or system access  

**Benefits**
- Increased awareness of security weaknesses can drive improvements  
- Encourages adoption of protective controls and monitoring  

**Drawbacks**
- Loss of sensitive information  
- Service disruption  
- Damage to organisational reputation  

---

## Privacy breaches

### Personal information  
### Business information  

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Exposure or misuse of stored data  
- Failure to protect confidential records  

**Benefits**
- Highlights importance of data governance and controls  

**Drawbacks**
- Loss of trust  
- Legal consequences  
- Financial penalties and compensation costs  

---

## Regulatory and legal non-compliance

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Failure to meet legislative or regulatory requirements  
- Inadequate policies, processes, or controls  

**Benefits**
- Drives organisations to improve compliance frameworks  

**Drawbacks**
- Fines and legal action  
- Withdrawal of licence to operate  
- Increased administrative burden  

---

## Audience exclusion

### Biases  
### Poor user experience  

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Digital systems fail to meet diverse user needs  
- Design or data introduces unintended bias  

**Benefits**
- Identifies areas for improvement in accessibility and usability  

**Drawbacks**
- Reduced user engagement  
- Reputational damage  
- Loss of potential customers or clients  

---

## Emerging rival technologies

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- New technologies provide alternative or improved solutions  
- Existing systems become less competitive  

**Benefits**
- Encourages innovation and system review  

**Drawbacks**
- Reduced market position  
- Pressure to invest in new platforms  
- Risk of obsolescence  

---

## Technical issues

### Reliance and system failure  
### System not fit for purpose  

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Dependence on digital infrastructure  
- Systems fail to meet operational requirements  

**Benefits**
- Promotes resilience planning and system evaluation  

**Drawbacks**
- Operational disruption  
- Productivity loss  
- Increased recovery and maintenance costs  

---

## Judgement

Suitability depends on:

i. effectiveness of security and privacy controls  
ii. ability to meet regulatory requirements  
iii. inclusivity and quality of user experience  
iv. organisational readiness to respond to technological change  
v. resilience of systems and supporting infrastructure  

Digital systems introduce efficiency and capability, but expose organisations to operational, legal, and reputational risk. Decisions must balance innovation against constraints such as cost, compliance obligations, technical reliability, and ongoing management.

---

!!! tip "Exam tip"
    Avoid listing risks without explanation. Marks are gained by linking each risk to organisational impact and suitability. Responses must connect features (for example confidentiality or availability) to consequences and constraints before making judgement.
