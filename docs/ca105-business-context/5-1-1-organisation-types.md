## Purpose and sectors of organisations

This section covers the purpose of organisations and the main organisational sectors, including private, public and voluntary/charity, and how these structures support digital activity within a business context.

---

## Purpose of the organisation

### Providing a service

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Delivers intangible outputs such as support, access, or expertise  
- Often centred on user experience, responsiveness, and ongoing engagement  
- Value is created through performance, reliability, and availability  

**Benefits**
- Enables recurring relationships with users  
- Can adapt services rapidly to changing needs  
- Often lower physical production costs  

**Drawbacks**
- Quality can be harder to measure consistently  
- Service delivery is dependent on staff availability and skills  
- Perceived value may vary between users  

---

### Providing a product

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Produces tangible or digital goods  
- Focuses on design, development, manufacture, or distribution  
- Value is created through ownership or licensed use  

**Benefits**
- Clear unit-based revenue models  
- Products can be replicated at scale  
- Easier to standardise quality  

**Drawbacks**
- Higher upfront development or production costs  
- Stock management and distribution introduce risk  
- Products may become obsolete over time  

---

## Private sector

### Small or medium-sized enterprise (SME)

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Smaller workforce and turnover  
- Often locally or regionally focused  
- Flexible organisational structure  

**Benefits**
- Faster decision-making  
- Greater adaptability to change  
- Closer customer relationships  

**Drawbacks**
- Limited financial and technical resources  
- Higher vulnerability to market changes  
- Reduced capacity for large-scale projects  

---

### Large enterprise

**Features / Characteristics /Role**
- Large workforce and operational scale  
- Structured management and departments  
- Often operates nationally or internationally  

**Benefits**
- Greater access to funding and infrastructure  
- Ability to invest in large digital systems  
- Higher operational resilience  

**Drawbacks**
- Slower decision-making  
- Increased organisational complexity  
- Higher costs of change management  

---

### Non-governmental organisation (NGO)

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Operates independently of government  
- Typically mission-driven  
- May operate across national boundaries  

**Benefits**
- Focus on social or environmental objectives  
- Can attract external funding or grants  
- Often trusted by communities  

**Drawbacks**
- Reliant on donations or funding cycles  
- Limited commercial revenue  
- Resource constraints can affect sustainability  

---

## Public sector

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Funded by taxation  
- Provides services for citizens  
- Governed by legislation and policy  

**Benefits**
- Stable funding models  
- Wide reach across populations  
- Strong accountability frameworks  

**Drawbacks**
- Budgetary constraints  
- Slower adoption of change  
- High regulatory overhead  

---

## Voluntary/charity (not for profit)

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Operates for social benefit rather than profit  
- Income derived from donations, grants, or fundraising  
- Surpluses are reinvested into organisational aims  

**Benefits**
- Strong ethical focus  
- High levels of community engagement  
- Eligibility for charitable funding  

**Drawbacks**
- Financial instability  
- Dependence on volunteers or donations  
- Limited capacity for long-term investment  

---

## Judgement

Suitability depends on:

i. organisational purpose (service delivery versus product provision)  
ii. scale of operation and available resources  
iii. funding model and financial sustainability  
iv. governance and regulatory constraints  
v. capacity to adopt and maintain digital systems  

Different sectors balance operational flexibility, financial security, and accountability in different ways. Digital software development decisions must account for these constraints when selecting platforms, managing change, and allocating resources.

---

!!! tip "Exam tip"
    Marks are awarded for linking organisational purpose and sector to impact and suitability. Avoid listing sectors in isolation — features must be connected to benefits, drawbacks, and organisational constraints to support judgement-based responses.
