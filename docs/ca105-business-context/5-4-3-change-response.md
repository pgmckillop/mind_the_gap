# 5.4.3 

## Organisational responses to change

This section covers how organisations respond to change through updates to policies, processes, products and services, digital systems, training, and organisational structure.

---

## New or amended policies

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Formal updates to rules, procedures, and standards  
- Define acceptable practice and operational direction  

**Benefits**
- Provides clarity and consistency  
- Supports compliance and accountability  

**Drawbacks**
- Requires staff awareness and adherence  
- Can increase administrative overhead  

---

## New or amended business processes

### Change in staffing numbers  
### Change in delivery schedules  
### Change in opening hours  

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Adjustments to how work is organised and delivered  
- Aligns operations with changing demand or resources  

**Benefits**
- Improves efficiency  
- Enables flexible service provision  
- Supports cost control  

**Drawbacks**
- Disruption to staff and customers  
- Risk of reduced service quality during transition  

---

## New or amended products or services

### Completely new products or services  
### Next generation products or services  
### Minor updates to existing products or services  

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Introduces innovation or incremental improvement  
- Responds to market demand or competitive pressure  

**Benefits**
- Maintains organisational relevance  
- Attracts new users or retains existing ones  
- Supports revenue growth  

**Drawbacks**
- Development and launch costs  
- Risk of poor adoption  
- Increased support requirements  

---

## New or improved digital systems

### Back-end systems  
### Customer-facing systems  

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Enhances internal operations or user interaction  
- Enables automation and data-driven processes  

**Benefits**
- Increased efficiency  
- Improved user experience  
- Better integration across organisational functions  

**Drawbacks**
- Implementation complexity  
- Training and maintenance costs  
- Potential disruption during deployment  

---

## Improved training

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Develops staff skills to support new systems or processes  
- Enables effective adoption of change  

**Benefits**
- Increases competence and confidence  
- Reduces errors  
- Supports long-term sustainability  

**Drawbacks**
- Time and cost investment  
- Temporary reduction in productivity  

---

## Restructuring

### Change in management structure  
### Redrawing of boundaries  

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Alters reporting lines and organisational responsibilities  
- Realigns teams and functions  

**Benefits**
- Clarifies accountability  
- Improves coordination  
- Supports strategic objectives  

**Drawbacks**
- Staff uncertainty  
- Resistance to change  
- Short-term operational disruption  

---

## Judgement

Suitability depends on:

i. scale and urgency of the required change  
ii. availability of financial and human resources  
iii. impact on existing operations and users  
iv. capability of staff to adapt  
v. effectiveness of communication and implementation  

Organisations respond to change through coordinated adjustments to policy, processes, systems, and structure. Digital software development must support these responses while balancing cost, disruption, and long-term sustainability.

---

!!! tip "Exam tip"
    Avoid listing response methods without explanation. Marks are awarded for linking organisational actions to impact and suitability. Responses must evaluate benefits and drawbacks of policies, processes, systems, and restructuring before making judgement.
