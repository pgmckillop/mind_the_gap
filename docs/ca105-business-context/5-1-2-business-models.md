## Business models

This section covers the names and definitions of different business models, specifically Business to Customer (B2C), Business to Business (B2B), and Business to Many (B2M), and how each model shapes organisational relationships, delivery methods, and digital system requirements.

---

## Business to Customer (B2C)

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Organisation sells products or services directly to individual customers  
- Typically involves high transaction volumes  
- Digital systems focus on user experience, accessibility, and payment processing  

**Benefits**
- Direct access to customers  
- Faster feedback on products or services  
- Opportunities for personalisation and targeted marketing  

**Drawbacks**
- High competition  
- Strong dependency on customer satisfaction and brand reputation  
- Requires scalable customer-facing digital systems  

---

## Business to Business (B2B)

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Organisation provides products or services to other organisations  
- Usually involves fewer customers but higher-value transactions  
- Digital systems often support contracts, integrations, and long-term relationships  

**Benefits**
- More predictable revenue streams  
- Stronger client relationships  
- Larger transaction values  

**Drawbacks**
- Longer sales cycles  
- Higher dependency on a smaller number of clients  
- Greater complexity in technical integration and compliance  

---

## Business to Many (B2M)

**Features / Characteristics / Role**
- Organisation delivers products or services to multiple organisations or groups simultaneously  
- Often platform-based  
- Digital systems manage distribution, access control, and scalability  

**Benefits**
- Broad market reach  
- Efficient distribution to multiple recipients  
- Supports rapid growth through shared platforms  

**Drawbacks**
- Increased system complexity  
- Higher infrastructure demands  
- More challenging to manage diverse user requirements  

---

## Judgement

Suitability depends on:

i. target audience (individual users versus organisations)  
ii. transaction volume and value  
iii. required level of system scalability  
iv. complexity of relationships and integrations  
v. resource availability and infrastructure capacity  

Each model places different demands on digital software development in terms of performance, security, and system design. Selection is constrained by organisational goals, technical capability, and operational scale.

---

!!! tip "Exam tip"
    Avoid listing B2C, B2B, and B2M without explanation. Marks are gained by linking model features to impacts on digital systems and organisational suitability. Definitions alone are insufficient — connect purpose, benefits, drawbacks, and constraints to support judgement.
