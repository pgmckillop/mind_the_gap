## End user characteristics and inclusivity of digital systems

This section covers how end user characteristics affect the use of digital systems and their inclusivity, including age, skills, audience type, cultural issues and additional needs.

---

## Age

**Features / Characteristics**
- Different age groups interact with digital systems in varied ways  
- Levels of familiarity with technology differ across generations  

**Benefits**
- Systems designed with age variation in mind can reach wider audiences  
- Improved usability across demographic groups  

**Drawbacks**
- Interfaces may disadvantage very young or older users  
- Increased design complexity to accommodate multiple age groups  

---

## Skills

### Digital skills

**Features / Characteristics**
- Ability to operate devices, applications and online services  

**Benefits**
- Enables effective use of digital systems  
- Supports independent interaction with services  

**Drawbacks**
- Low digital skills limit access  
- Increased reliance on support mechanisms  

---

### Literacy

**Features / Characteristics**
- Ability to read, understand and interpret written content  

**Benefits**
- Supports accurate interaction with system content  
- Enables completion of digital tasks  

**Drawbacks**
- Poor literacy restricts system usability  
- Risk of misunderstanding instructions or outputs  

---

## Internal and external audience

**Features / Characteristics**
- Internal users operate within an organisation  
- External users interact as customers or members of the public  

**Benefits**
- Targeted design improves relevance and efficiency  
- Supports role-appropriate access and functionality  

**Drawbacks**
- Differing expectations increase design and maintenance effort  
- Risk of exclusion if one audience group is prioritised  

---

## Cultural issues, including bias in digital systems

**Features / Characteristics**
- Cultural norms influence how systems are perceived and used  
- Bias may be embedded through datasets or design choices  

**Benefits**
- Inclusive design supports wider participation  
- Reduces unfair outcomes  

**Drawbacks**
- Identifying bias requires specialist skills  
- Cultural assumptions can unintentionally exclude users  

---

## Additional needs

### Accessibility issues

**Features / Characteristics**
- Users may require adjustments to visual, auditory, motor or cognitive interaction  

**Benefits**
- Improved access for users with additional needs  
- Wider reach of digital services  

**Drawbacks**
- Increased development and testing effort  
- Additional resource requirements  

---

## Judgement

Suitability of digital systems depends on:

i. age-related usability requirements  
ii. levels of digital skill and literacy  
iii. differences between internal and external audiences  
iv. cultural sensitivity and mitigation of bias  
v. availability of resources to support accessibility  

Inclusive systems improve reach and usability but are constrained by development complexity, cost, skills availability and data quality. Effective implementation balances accessibility requirements with organisational capacity and technical feasibility.

---

!!! tip "Exam tip"
Marks are awarded for explaining how **user characteristics affect usability and inclusion**, not for listing traits. Link characteristics to system impact and constraints such as skills, resources and bias. Judgement must balance accessibility benefits against implementation limitations.
