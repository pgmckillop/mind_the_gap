## Increased reliance on digital systems

This section covers how increased reliance on digital systems impacts organisational culture and society.

---

## Organisational culture

### Changes in communication methods (face to face, email, video calls)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Shift from in-person communication to digital channels
- Increased use of asynchronous and remote communication tools

**Benefits**
- Faster information sharing  
- Greater flexibility in working location  
- Wider participation across geographically dispersed teams  

**Drawbacks**
- Reduced face-to-face interaction  
- Increased risk of miscommunication  
- Potential weakening of workplace relationships  

---

### Increased productivity and availability expectations

**Features / Characteristics**
- Continuous connectivity
- Expectation of rapid responses and extended availability

**Benefits**
- Improved task throughput  
- Faster decision cycles  
- Increased organisational output  

**Drawbacks**
- Work–life balance pressure  
- Risk of burnout  
- Blurred boundaries between personal and professional time  

---

### Increase in staff monitoring

**Features / Characteristics**
- Digital tracking of activity, performance and attendance

**Benefits**
- Improved accountability  
- Easier identification of productivity trends  
- Enhanced compliance monitoring  

**Drawbacks**
- Reduced employee trust  
- Perceived loss of autonomy  
- Increased stress and disengagement  

---

### New working practices (remote/hybrid/in-office working)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Flexible work arrangements enabled by digital platforms

**Benefits**
- Wider recruitment opportunities  
- Reduced commuting  
- Improved flexibility for staff  

**Drawbacks**
- Potential isolation  
- Reduced team cohesion  
- Dependence on reliable connectivity and equipment  

---

### Automation of services including the use of artificial intelligence (AI)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Replacement or augmentation of manual tasks with automated systems
- Increased use of AI-driven processes

**Benefits**
- Greater operational efficiency  
- Reduced repetitive workload  
- Consistent service delivery  

**Drawbacks**
- Job displacement  
- Reduced human oversight  
- Dependence on automated decision-making  

---

## Society

### Loss of jobs

**Features / Characteristics**
- Automation replacing human roles

**Benefits**
- Increased efficiency in service delivery  
- Creation of new technical roles  

**Drawbacks**
- Unemployment in affected sectors  
- Economic disruption for individuals and communities  

---

### Shift in skill requirements

**Features / Characteristics**
- Growing demand for digital and technical skills

**Benefits**
- Development of new career pathways  
- Increased employability for digitally skilled individuals  

**Drawbacks**
- Skills gaps  
- Exclusion of those unable to retrain  

---

### Reduction in human decision making and loss of empathy

**Features / Characteristics**
- Algorithm-driven outcomes replacing human judgement

**Benefits**
- Faster, data-driven decisions  
- Reduced inconsistency  

**Drawbacks**
- Loss of contextual understanding  
- Reduced empathy in services  

---

### Privacy (digital footprint, surveillance)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Collection and monitoring of personal data

**Benefits**
- Improved security and service personalisation  

**Drawbacks**
- Reduced personal privacy  
- Increased risk of misuse of data  

---

### Changing behaviours (loss of social skills, digital identity)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Increased online interaction shaping personal identity

**Benefits**
- Expanded online engagement  
- New forms of social connection  

**Drawbacks**
- Reduced face-to-face communication skills  
- Identity risks in digital environments  

---

### Access to wider social networks (personal and professional)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Digital platforms enabling broader connections

**Benefits**
- Increased collaboration opportunities  
- Wider professional reach  

**Drawbacks**
- Superficial relationships  
- Exposure to misinformation  

---

### Access to online services (government, commercial and entertainment)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Services delivered primarily through digital platforms

**Benefits**
- Convenience and speed of access  
- Increased service availability  

**Drawbacks**
- Exclusion of digitally disadvantaged groups  
- Dependence on connectivity  

---

### Potential isolation (lack of skill, equipment, connectivity, resistance to change)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Individuals unable or unwilling to engage digitally

**Benefits**
- Highlights need for inclusion strategies  

**Drawbacks**
- Social exclusion  
- Reduced access to essential services  

---

### Improved access to information (professional and personal)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Immediate access to large volumes of digital content

**Benefits**
- Enhanced learning opportunities  
- Faster knowledge sharing  

**Drawbacks**
- Information overload  
- Difficulty verifying accuracy  

---

### Increased use of AI including generative AI (textual, graphical, video and audio)

**Features / Characteristics**
- AI producing content across multiple media types

**Benefits**
- Increased productivity  
- Creative and analytical support  

**Drawbacks**
- Authenticity concerns  
- Risk of dependency on generated content  

---

### Globalisation (access to global media sources)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Worldwide access to digital content and services

**Benefits**
- Broader cultural awareness  
- Expanded market reach  

**Drawbacks**
- Cultural homogenisation  
- Increased competition  

---

## Judgement

Suitability of increased reliance on digital systems depends on:

i. availability of skills, equipment and connectivity  
ii. organisational capacity to manage monitoring, automation and wellbeing  
iii. effectiveness of data protection and privacy controls  
iv. ability to support inclusion and mitigate social isolation  
v. balance between efficiency gains and human-centred decision making  

Digital systems enable productivity, connectivity and access to information, but introduce risks relating to employment, privacy, wellbeing and social cohesion. Outcomes are constrained by workforce readiness, infrastructure, governance and societal adaptation.

---

!!! tip "Exam tip"
Marks are awarded for linking **features → impact → suitability**. Avoid listing effects in isolation. Organise responses clearly under *organisational culture* and *society*, and balance benefits against drawbacks. Judgement statements must reference constraints such as skills, connectivity, privacy and workforce impact.
