# 7.6.2 Methods to improve resilience of digital environments

This section covers methods used to improve the resilience of digital environments.

---

## Software updates and upgrades

### Planned updates and upgrades

**Features**

- Scheduled installation of new software versions  

**Benefits**

- Maintains system stability  
- Introduces performance improvements and security enhancements  

**Drawbacks**

- Planned downtime may be required  
- Compatibility issues can arise  

---

### Patches in response to new vulnerabilities

**Features**

- Targeted fixes released to address specific security issues  

**Benefits**

- Reduces exposure to emerging threats  
- Supports continued secure operation  

**Drawbacks**

- Requires rapid deployment  
- May introduce unexpected side effects  

---

## Hardware replacement

### Rolling replacement plans

**Features**

- Gradual replacement of ageing equipment  

**Benefits**

- Reduces risk of sudden hardware failure  
- Spreads cost over time  

**Drawbacks**

- Mixed hardware environments increase complexity  

---

### Secure disposal

**Features**

- Safe removal of data from retired hardware  

**Benefits**

- Prevents data leakage  
- Supports compliance  

**Drawbacks**

- Requires controlled processes and oversight  

---

## Data and system redundancy

### Features

- Duplication of critical systems and data  

### Benefits

- Maintains availability during failures  
- Supports rapid recovery  

### Drawbacks

- Increased cost  
- Additional management overhead  

---

## Device hardening

### Features

- Removal of unneeded ports, applications, permissions, and access  

### Benefits

- Reduces attack surface  
- Limits potential misuse  

### Drawbacks

- May restrict functionality  
- Requires careful configuration  

---

## Backup systems and recovery procedures

### Onsite

**Benefits**

- Fast local recovery  

**Drawbacks**

- Vulnerable to site-wide incidents  

---

### Remote / offsite

**Benefits**

- Protects against local disasters  

**Drawbacks**

- Slower restoration times  

---

### Cloud

**Benefits**

- Scalable storage  
- Accessible from multiple locations  

**Drawbacks**

- Dependent on connectivity  
- Ongoing service costs  

---

## Hot, warm, and cold sites

### Hot sites

**Features**

- Fully operational standby environments  

**Benefits**

- Minimal downtime  

**Drawbacks**

- High cost  

---

### Warm sites

**Features**

- Partially prepared environments  

**Benefits**

- Balanced recovery time and cost  

**Drawbacks**

- Some setup required before use  

---

### Cold sites

**Features**

- Basic facilities without active systems  

**Benefits**

- Lowest cost  

**Drawbacks**

- Longest recovery time  

---

## Standard operating procedures

### Effective staff training

- Ensures correct response to incidents  

### Induction

- Introduces resilience practices to new staff  

### New digital systems

- Provides guidance during system changes  

### New or updated policies

- Maintains consistent operational standards  

**Benefits**

- Reduces human error  
- Supports consistent recovery processes  

**Drawbacks**

- Requires ongoing review and reinforcement  

---

## Judgement

Resilience is improved through a combination of technical controls, redundancy, proactive maintenance, and operational procedures.

Suitability depends on:

i. organisational risk tolerance  
ii. budget and resource availability  
iii. acceptable recovery times  
iv. scale of operations  

Effective resilience requires balancing cost and complexity against security, availability, and continuity.

---

!!! tip "Exam tip"
    Higher marks are gained by linking **each method** (for example redundancy, backups, or patching) to its **impact on resilience**, and by comparing hot, warm, and cold sites using recovery time versus cost. Avoid listing techniques without explaining their role.
