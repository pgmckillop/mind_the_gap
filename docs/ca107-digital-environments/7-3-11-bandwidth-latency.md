# 7.3.11 Bandwidth and latency

This section covers the concepts of bandwidth and latency and how they affect the performance of networks and connected systems.

---

## Bandwidth

### Concept

- Bandwidth is the maximum amount of data that can be transmitted over a network connection in a given time period  

### Effect on performance

- Higher bandwidth allows more data to be transferred simultaneously  
- Low bandwidth limits throughput and increases transfer times  

### Benefits

- Supports high-volume data transfer  
- Improves performance for multiple connected devices  

### Drawbacks

- Higher bandwidth connections often increase cost  
- Inefficient use of bandwidth can still result in poor performance  

---

## Latency

### Concept

- Latency is the time taken for data to travel from source to destination  

### Effect on performance

- Low latency enables faster responses  
- High latency causes delays, even when bandwidth is high  

### Benefits

- Low latency improves responsiveness of systems  
- Supports real-time communication and interaction  

### Drawbacks

- High latency reduces user experience and system efficiency  
- Can affect time-sensitive applications  

---

## Combined impact on network performance

Bandwidth and latency work together to determine overall performance:

- High bandwidth with high latency allows large transfers but slow responses  
- Low latency with low bandwidth gives fast responses but limited data capacity  

Effective network performance depends on balancing both.

---

## Judgement

Network and system performance is determined by the relationship between bandwidth and latency. Increasing bandwidth improves data capacity, while reducing latency improves responsiveness.

Suitability depends on:

i. volume of transmitted data  
ii. responsiveness requirements  
iii. number of connected systems  
iv. cost constraints  

Optimising one without considering the other can limit overall effectiveness.

---

!!! tip "Exam tip"
    Remember: **bandwidth = amount of data**, **latency = delay**. Higher marks are gained by explaining how both together affect performance, not by defining them separately.
