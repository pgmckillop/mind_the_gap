## Connectivity methods

This section covers the features, characteristics, benefits, and drawbacks of wired and wireless connectivity methods.

---

## Wired connectivity

### Copper / Ethernet

### Features and characteristics

- Uses copper cabling to transmit electrical signals  
- Commonly implemented using Ethernet standards  
- Requires physical cabling between devices  

### Benefits

- Reliable and stable connection  
- Consistent performance  
- Less susceptible to interference  

### Drawbacks

- Limited maximum cable length  
- Installation requires physical infrastructure  
- Reduced flexibility compared with wireless solutions  

---

### Fibre-optic

### Features and characteristics

- Uses light signals transmitted through optical fibre  
- Supports very high data transfer rates  
- Suitable for long-distance connections  

### Benefits

- High bandwidth  
- Low signal loss over long distances  
- Resistant to electromagnetic interference  

### Drawbacks

- Higher installation cost  
- More complex to repair or modify  
- Requires specialised equipment  

---

## Wireless connectivity

### Wireless access points

### Features and characteristics

- Provide wireless network access to devices  
- Connect wireless devices to a wired network  
- Use radio signals instead of physical cables  

### Benefits

- Increased mobility and flexibility  
- Reduced need for physical cabling  
- Supports portable and mobile devices  

### Drawbacks

- Susceptible to interference  
- Generally lower performance than wired connections  
- Security risks if not configured correctly  

---

## Judgement

Selection of connectivity methods depends on:

i. performance requirements  
ii. distance and physical environment  
iii. installation and infrastructure constraints  
iv. mobility needs  
v. cost and maintenance considerations  

Copper/Ethernet provides reliable short-range connectivity, fibre-optic supports high-speed long-distance communication, and wireless access points enable flexible access. Each method involves trade-offs between speed, reliability, flexibility, security, and installation complexity.

---

!!! tip "Exam tip"
    Marks are awarded for explaining **features and characteristics** alongside **benefits and drawbacks**. Higher credit is gained by comparing wired and wireless methods and justifying suitability based on performance, distance, and flexibility.
