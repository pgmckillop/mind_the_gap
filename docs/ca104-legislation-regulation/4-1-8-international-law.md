## Application of international law to digital offences

This section covers how international law applies to certain digital offences, including activity in cyberspace and surveillance practices.

---

## International law in cyberspace

**Features / Characteristics**
- Digital offences may cross national borders  
- Jurisdiction can involve multiple countries  
- Cooperation between international authorities is required to investigate and prosecute cybercrime  

**Benefits**
- Enables action against cross-border criminal activity  
- Supports shared standards for cyber conduct  
- Improves global coordination in responding to digital threats  

**Drawbacks**
- Complex legal processes  
- Differing national laws and enforcement priorities  
- Slow investigation and resolution timelines  

---

## International law and surveillance

**Features / Characteristics**
- Surveillance activities may involve data collected or stored across jurisdictions  
- Legal frameworks govern when and how monitoring can occur  

**Benefits**
- Supports national and international security  
- Provides legal oversight of surveillance practices  
- Helps protect citizens through regulated monitoring  

**Drawbacks**
- Privacy concerns  
- Risk of inconsistent protections between countries  
- Limited transparency for individuals  

---

## Judgement

Suitability of applying international law to digital offences depends on:

i. cooperation between nations and enforcement bodies  
ii. alignment of legal frameworks across jurisdictions  
iii. availability of technical and investigative expertise  
iv. balance between security requirements and individual privacy  
v. effectiveness of oversight and accountability mechanisms  

International law enables coordinated responses to cybercrime and surveillance but is constrained by jurisdictional complexity, legal variation and enforcement capability. Impact on organisations, society and individuals is shaped by how effectively global cooperation balances security with civil liberties.

---

!!! tip "Exam tip"
Marks are awarded for linking **cross-border digital activity → legal response → impact on organisations and individuals**. Avoid simply naming international law. In Judgement, reference constraints such as jurisdiction, cooperation and privacy when evaluating suitability.
