# 4.1.5 Understand equality legislation

This section covers equality legislation, including the nine protected characteristics, types of discrimination, where individuals are protected, and when action must be taken against discrimination, including time limits for claims.

---

## Nine protected characteristics

**Features / Characteristics / Role**

Equality legislation identifies nine protected characteristics:

i. age  
ii. disability  
iii. gender reassignment  
iv. marriage and civil partnership  
v. pregnancy and maternity  
vi. race  
vii. religion or belief  
viii. sex  
ix. sexual orientation  

These characteristics define the groups legally protected from unfair treatment and guide organisational policies, recruitment practices and workplace behaviour.

**Benefits**

- provides legal protection against unfair treatment  
- promotes inclusive organisational cultures  
- supports equal access to employment and services  
- establishes clear accountability for discriminatory behaviour  

**Drawbacks**

- requires formal policies and monitoring processes  
- increases compliance and training requirements  
- disputes may result in legal and administrative costs  

---

## Types of discrimination

**Features / Characteristics / Role**

Equality legislation recognises the following forms of discrimination:

i. **direct** – treating someone less favourably because of a protected characteristic  
ii. **indirect** – applying policies or practices that disadvantage protected groups  
iii. **harassment** – unwanted conduct related to a protected characteristic that violates dignity or creates a hostile environment  
iv. **victimisation** – unfair treatment resulting from raising or supporting a complaint  

These definitions establish behavioural boundaries and inform disciplinary and grievance procedures.

**Benefits**

- clarifies unacceptable conduct  
- supports consistent handling of complaints  
- strengthens workplace standards  

**Drawbacks**

- interpretation can be complex  
- investigations may be resource-intensive  
- may increase organisational risk exposure during disputes  

---

## Where individuals are protected

**Features / Characteristics / Role**

Protection applies across:

- employment and recruitment  
- education and training  
- provision of goods and services  
- access to public services  

Organisations must ensure fair treatment throughout digital system design, service delivery and workplace practices.

**Benefits**

- ensures broad legal coverage  
- promotes fairness across multiple environments  
- reinforces trust in organisational processes  

**Drawbacks**

- requires comprehensive policy implementation  
- increases organisational responsibility across operational areas  

---

## When to take action against discrimination

### Time limits for claims

**Features / Characteristics / Role**

Individuals must raise claims within defined legal timeframes following discriminatory behaviour. These limits ensure timely reporting, investigation and resolution.

**Benefits**

- encourages prompt action  
- supports evidence gathering  
- reduces prolonged disputes  

**Drawbacks**

- individuals may miss deadlines due to lack of awareness  
- late reporting may prevent formal resolution  

---

## Judgement

Suitability of equality legislation in digital software development depends on:

i. organisational capability to implement inclusive policies and training  
ii. management of complaints and reporting mechanisms  
iii. availability of resources to support compliance  
iv. integration of equality considerations into recruitment, system design and service delivery  

While equality legislation provides structured protection and accountability, its effectiveness relies on consistent enforcement, staff awareness and organisational commitment.
