## Sources of digital industry standards

This section covers the main sources of digital industry standards and their role in defining technical, accessibility, security and operational expectations within digital software development.

---

## International Organization for Standardization (ISO)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Produces internationally recognised standards across technology and business
- Focus on quality, safety, interoperability and management systems

**Benefits**
- Global consistency  
- Supports interoperability between systems  
- Improves organisational credibility  

**Drawbacks**
- Certification can be costly  
- Standards may be high level and require interpretation  
- Updates may lag behind emerging technologies  

---

## Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Accessibility standards for web and digital content
- Focus on inclusive design and usability

**Benefits**
- Improves access for users with additional needs  
- Supports legal compliance  
- Broadens audience reach  

**Drawbacks**
- Increases development and testing effort  
- Requires specialist accessibility knowledge  

---

## World Wide Web Consortium (W3C®)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Develops standards for web technologies and protocols
- Promotes consistency across browsers and platforms

**Benefits**
- Improves compatibility  
- Supports long-term maintainability  
- Encourages open web development  

**Drawbacks**
- Adoption varies across platforms  
- Implementation may increase complexity  

---

## Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Develops standards for internet protocols and networking

**Benefits**
- Enables reliable global communication  
- Supports interoperability of networked systems  

**Drawbacks**
- Highly technical documentation  
- Requires specialist networking knowledge  

---

## British Standard (BS)

**Features / Characteristics**
- UK-specific standards covering technology and operational practices

**Benefits**
- Supports national consistency  
- Aligns with regulatory expectations  

**Drawbacks**
- Limited international scope  
- May overlap with global standards  

---

## Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Produces standards for hardware, software and networking technologies

**Benefits**
- Supports technical precision  
- Widely adopted in engineering and computing  

**Drawbacks**
- Can be complex to implement  
- May require paid access to documentation  

---

## Payment Card Industry Security Standards Council (PCI SSC)

**Features / Characteristics**
- Defines security standards for payment card data

**Benefits**
- Reduces risk of financial data breaches  
- Improves trust in digital payment systems  

**Drawbacks**
- Compliance can be resource intensive  
- Ongoing auditing requirements  

---

## Judgement

Suitability of digital industry standards depends on:

i. organisational capacity to implement and maintain compliance  
ii. relevance of each standard to the system being developed  
iii. availability of technical expertise  
iv. cost of certification and auditing  
v. need for national versus international alignment  

Industry standards improve quality, security and interoperability but introduce complexity and administrative overhead. Their impact is constrained by resources, skills and the pace of technological change.
