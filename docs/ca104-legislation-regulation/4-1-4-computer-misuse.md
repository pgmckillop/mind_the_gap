# 4.1.4 Understand computer misuse legislation

This section covers computer misuse legislation, focusing on the principles of the Computer Misuse Act (CMA) 1990, the consequences for companies and employees, employee awareness, and the types of crime covered by the legislation.

---

## Principles of the Computer Misuse Act (CMA) 1990

**Features / Characteristics / Role**

The CMA establishes offences relating to unauthorised access to computer systems and data. Its core principles include:

i. unauthorised access to computer material  
ii. unauthorised access with intent to commit or facilitate further offences  
iii. unauthorised acts intended to impair the operation of computers or data  
iv. unauthorised acts causing, or creating risk of, serious damage  

These principles provide a legal framework to protect systems, networks and data from misuse and malicious activity.

**Benefits**

- deters illegal access to digital systems  
- supports prosecution of cyber-related offences  
- reinforces the need for access controls and security policies  
- protects organisational assets and data integrity  

**Drawbacks**

- relies on effective detection and reporting mechanisms  
- enforcement can be complex across digital environments  
- compliance introduces additional monitoring and security costs  

---

## Consequences for company and employee

**Features / Characteristics / Role**

- organisations may face legal action, fines, reputational damage and loss of client trust  
- employees may face disciplinary action, dismissal and criminal prosecution  
- breaches can lead to operational disruption and increased regulatory scrutiny  

**Benefits**

- encourages organisations to implement stronger security controls  
- reinforces accountability for individuals  
- promotes responsible behaviour when accessing systems  

**Drawbacks**

- investigations can be time-consuming and costly  
- reputational damage may persist even after resolution  
- employees may feel increased surveillance in tightly controlled environments  

---

## Employee awareness

**Features / Characteristics / Role**

- staff must understand acceptable use policies and access permissions  
- awareness training supports recognition of unlawful activity  
- clear reporting procedures enable early identification of misuse  

**Benefits**

- reduces accidental breaches  
- strengthens organisational security culture  
- supports compliance with legal and internal requirements  

**Drawbacks**

- requires ongoing training investment  
- effectiveness depends on staff engagement and understanding  

---

## Types of crime covered by legislation

**Features / Characteristics / Role**

The CMA applies to offences including:

i. unauthorised system access  
ii. unauthorised modification or deletion of data  
iii. denial of service and system disruption  
iv. creation or deployment of malicious software  
v. activities intended to cause serious damage to systems or information  

These offences address both deliberate attacks and actions that significantly compromise system availability, integrity or confidentiality.

**Benefits**

- provides legal protection against a wide range of cyber threats  
- supports consistent treatment of digital crime  
- reinforces the importance of secure system design  

**Drawbacks**

- evolving attack methods can outpace legislation  
- proving intent and attribution can be difficult  

---

## Judgement

Suitability of computer misuse legislation in digital software development depends on:

i. effectiveness of organisational security controls and monitoring  
ii. staff awareness and adherence to access policies  
iii. cost and resource availability for prevention and response  
iv. ability to adapt controls to emerging threat types  

While the CMA establishes clear boundaries for lawful system use, its impact is constrained by organisational implementation, staff behaviour and the pace of technological change.
