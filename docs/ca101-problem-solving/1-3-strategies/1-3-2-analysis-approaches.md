## 1.3.2 Know the benefits and drawbacks of using the different approaches to solving problems

This section covers the advantages and limitations of top-down, bottom-up and modularisation approaches when solving problems in digital software development.

## Top-down approach

### Features / Characteristics / Role

The top-down approach begins with the overall system and progressively decomposes it into smaller components for implementation.

### Benefits

- provides clear system-wide structure  
- supports early identification of requirements  
- improves coordination between components  
- enables planned development pathways  

### Drawbacks

- implementation issues may appear late  
- changes at higher levels affect multiple components  
- depends on accurate early analysis  
- can reduce flexibility during development  

---

## Bottom-up approach

### Features / Characteristics / Role

The bottom-up approach focuses on developing individual components first and integrating them into a complete solution.

### Benefits

- enables early testing of components  
- supports reuse of existing modules  
- allows flexible assembly of solutions  
- reduces reliance on complete system design at early stages  

### Drawbacks

- may lack overall system coherence  
- integration problems can emerge late  
- risks mismatched components  
- requires additional effort to align with system goals  

---

## Modularisation

### Features / Characteristics / Role

Modularisation structures solutions into independent modules that interact through defined interfaces.

### Benefits

- improves maintainability and scalability  
- supports parallel development  
- simplifies testing and debugging  
- enables reuse of components  

### Drawbacks

- requires careful design of module boundaries  
- introduces coordination and integration overhead  
- increases initial design effort  
- poorly defined modules reduce effectiveness  

---

## Judgement

Suitability depends on:

i. problem complexity and scale  
ii. clarity of requirements  
iii. availability of reusable components  
iv. time and resource constraints  
v. developer experience  

Top-down, bottom-up and modularisation approaches each offer different strengths. Selection depends on project priorities, integration requirements and the balance between early structure and development flexibility.
