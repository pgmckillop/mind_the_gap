# 2.6.1 Know and understand sequence, its purpose and when it is used

This section covers sequence as a control structure, its role in determining the order of execution, and when it is applied in digital software development.

## Sequence

### Features / Characteristics / Role

Sequence refers to the execution of program statements in a defined order, from the first instruction to the last. Each step is performed one after another unless altered by selection or iteration.

### Benefits

- provides predictable program flow  
- supports clear step-by-step processing  
- simplifies understanding of execution order  
- enables straightforward implementation of linear tasks  
- forms the foundation for more complex control structures  

### Drawbacks

- limited flexibility for handling alternative conditions  
- unsuitable for repeated or conditional operations on its own  
- errors early in the sequence affect all subsequent steps  

---

## Purpose of sequence

### Features / Characteristics / Role

Sequence is used to organise instructions so that operations occur in the required order, ensuring inputs are processed before outputs are produced and calculations occur at the correct point in execution.

### Benefits

- ensures logical progression of tasks  
- supports reliable data processing  
- enables accurate calculations and results  
- improves readability of program logic  

### Drawbacks

- rigid ordering can reduce adaptability  
- changes to early steps may require revision of later steps  

---

## When sequence is used

### Features / Characteristics / Role

Sequence is applied whenever instructions must be executed in a specific order, particularly for:

- processing input before performing calculations  
- assigning values before they are used  
- producing output after processing is complete  

### Benefits

- supports structured program design  
- enables predictable outcomes  

### Drawbacks

- becomes insufficient for complex logic without selection or iteration  

---

## Judgement

Suitability depends on:

i. simplicity of the task  
ii. need for conditional or repeated behaviour  
iii. clarity of execution order  
iv. complexity of the solution  
v. developer experience  

Sequence is most suitable for linear processes with a fixed execution order, but must be combined with selection and iteration to support more complex program behaviour.

---

!!! tip "Exam tip"
Marks are awarded for linking sequence to execution order. Avoid defining sequence alone — explain how ordered steps affect program flow, outcomes and suitability.
